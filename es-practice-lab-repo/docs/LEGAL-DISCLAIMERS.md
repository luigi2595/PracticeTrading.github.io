# Legal disclaimers

> **This file is a starting point, not legal advice.** It was drafted by
> assembling commonly used language; nobody qualified has reviewed it for this
> product. Before charging money — and ideally before a public launch — have a
> lawyer familiar with CFTC/NFA marketing rules review everything here and
> everything the app displays. Requirements also differ outside the US.

## Why this matters here

The app displays simulated trading results: P&L, win rate, expectancy in R,
equity curves. In the US, presenting hypothetical or simulated performance
pulls in disclosure expectations under CFTC Rule 4.41 and, for NFA members,
NFA Compliance Rule 2-29. The short version of the standard is that simulated
results must be clearly labelled as such and accompanied by a statement of
their limitations, so nobody mistakes them for an actual trading record.

Two other lines worth staying well clear of:

- **Do not give trading advice.** The app teaches how to read structure and
  manage risk. It should never tell a user what to buy or sell, and marketing
  should not imply it produces signals or profitable strategies.
- **Do not imply affiliation.** "E-mini" and CME product names belong to CME
  Group; "S&P 500" is a trademark of S&P Dow Jones Indices. Descriptive use
  ("a simulator for E-mini S&P 500 futures") is normally fine, but branding,
  logos, and domain names should not suggest endorsement. Worth a trademark
  check on the final product name.

## Hypothetical performance disclaimer (template)

The widely used formulation, which appears in the app and should appear
anywhere results are shown or marketed:

> HYPOTHETICAL OR SIMULATED PERFORMANCE RESULTS HAVE CERTAIN INHERENT
> LIMITATIONS. UNLIKE AN ACTUAL PERFORMANCE RECORD, SIMULATED RESULTS DO NOT
> REPRESENT ACTUAL TRADING. ALSO, SINCE THE TRADES HAVE NOT ACTUALLY BEEN
> EXECUTED, THE RESULTS MAY HAVE UNDER- OR OVER-COMPENSATED FOR THE IMPACT, IF
> ANY, OF CERTAIN MARKET FACTORS, SUCH AS LACK OF LIQUIDITY. SIMULATED TRADING
> PROGRAMS IN GENERAL ARE ALSO SUBJECT TO THE FACT THAT THEY ARE DESIGNED WITH
> THE BENEFIT OF HINDSIGHT. NO REPRESENTATION IS BEING MADE THAT ANY ACCOUNT
> WILL OR IS LIKELY TO ACHIEVE PROFIT OR LOSSES SIMILAR TO THOSE SHOWN.

A product-specific point that strengthens the disclosure: the default price
data here is not merely a simulated *record* of real prices — it is
**synthetic price generated by an algorithm**. It contains no real market
events. That should be stated plainly rather than buried.

## Risk disclosure (template)

> Futures trading involves substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for
> every investor. You may lose more than your initial deposit. Past
> performance, whether actual or simulated, is not indicative of future
> results. Nothing in this software constitutes trading, investment, financial,
> legal, or tax advice, and no part of it is a recommendation to buy or sell
> any instrument. You are solely responsible for your own trading decisions.

## No-affiliation disclosure (added for the prop-firm evaluation mode)

Now that the app enforces prop-firm-style evaluation rules (trailing
drawdown, profit target, daily loss limit, consistency), the same care that
applies to hypothetical-performance language applies here: never let a user
come away thinking a pass in this app is a pass with a real firm, or that
this product has any relationship with one. Standard line to reuse wherever
the feature is described:

> This app is independent practice software. It is not affiliated with,
> endorsed by, or sponsored by any proprietary trading firm. Nothing in it is
> a real funded account, and no result is submitted to or recognized by any
> evaluation program. Passing the simulated rules here does not mean you will
> pass — or are likely to pass — a real firm's evaluation; always confirm that
> firm's actual current rules directly with them before paying for an attempt.

This is where the 2026 regulatory picture (see [BUSINESS.md](BUSINESS.md#open-risks))
bites hardest: the SEC's enforcement this year was specifically about
marketing that blurred a simulated evaluation account with live market
exposure. This product doesn't sell evaluations or take challenge fees, so it
isn't the target of that action or of the CFTC's commodity-pool consultation,
but a user who came away confused about what "passed" means here would be
exactly the kind of confusion those actions were about. Keep the distinction
explicit everywhere the feature is described, not just in this file.

## Where these appear in the app today

| Location | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome modal (first run, and the `?` button) | Synthetic data, not advice, futures can lose more than you deposit, the hypothetical-performance statement, and the no-affiliation line |
| Settings → "Prop firm evaluation" card | The no-affiliation / not-a-real-account disclosure, directly under the feature's on/off switch |
| Settings → "What this simulator does and does not teach" | Long-form explanation of what synthetic data can and cannot teach, and the hypothetical-performance statement |

| README | Prominent callout near the top |
